## Safety component

## Relevant section of the draft guidelines
**III.2.2 · Safety component**&nbsp;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**2. The main elements for classification as high-risk under Article 6(1) AI Act**  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**2.2. The AI system is a product itself, or a safety component of a product, covered by the Union harmonisation legislation**  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**2.2.2 Safety components**  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**(ii) Failure of malfunctioning endangering health and safety of persons or property**  

> **(48)** The AI Act does not classify any product in the scope of Article 6(1) automatically as high-risk. Most, but not all, AI systems in consumer ‘smart’ home appliances, such as AI systems in thermostats, washing machines, air purifiers, refrigerators, and televisions, will fall outside the definition of a safety component, since their intended purpose is to ensure comfort, convenience, or optimisation (e.g. energy use, air quality, brightness, sound), and a failure or malfunctioning of the system is unlikely to endanger health and safety of persons or property, but will rather lead to, for example, inconvenience, discomfort, incorrect suggestions and degraded performance.

## Comment
The opening sentence is ambiguous, since the legal object of classification is the AI system (not the product), even where the AI system is itself a product. The paragraph then goes on to explain that "most [...] AI systems in consumer 'smart' home appliances [...] will fall outside the definition of a safety component", which is one of the cumulative conditions under Article 6(1).  

It is unclear what is meant by "products in the scope of Article 6(1)", since Article 6(1) itself establishes the cumulative conditions under which AI systems are classified as high-risk. It would be helpful to clarify if the reference is to AI systems embedded in products covered by the Union harmonisation legislation, rather than products "in the scope of Article 6(1)".
