## Horizontal issues - Limitations in some use cases to natural persons

## Relevant section of the draft guidelines
**IV.2 · Horizontal issues**&nbsp;  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;**2.2. Limitations in some use cases to natural persons**  

> **(73)** A ‘natural person’ is distinct from a legal person. The notion of ‘natural person’ also includes, but is not restricted to, consumers. Sole traders, independent professions and other self-employed persons are also considered natural persons in relation to the commercial activity undertaken by 6 those persons. This includes, for example natural persons acting in a professional capacity (consultants, designers, journalists).  

> **Example**: A provider develops an AI system which is intended to establish the credit score of an owner of a small business or a company, which is not a legal entity. Such a system is not intended to evaluate a ‘natural person’ when it uses only business or company data.

## Comment
The paragraph clarifies that the notion of "natural person" includes sole traders, independent professionals and other self-employed persons acting in a professional capacity.  However, the accompanying example falls short of clearly illustrating the intended distinction.  

It states that an AI system establishing the credit score of an individual acting in one of those capacities would not be considered to evaluate a natural person where only business or company data are used, but it does not explain the rationale underlying this conclusion.  Consider specifying the types of data used as input (e.g. company turnover, payment history, corporate liabilities) to clarify why the assessment concerns the business rather than the individual.  

It would also be helpful to include a contrasting example illustrating when the evaluation of a sole trader or self-employed professional would instead be considered to evaluate a natural person (e.g. where the assessment relies on personal financial history, personal income or other personal characteristics).
